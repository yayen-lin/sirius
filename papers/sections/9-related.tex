\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Similarity joins}
The partitioned join of Section~\ref{sec:approx-join} has ancestors there: Lu et al.\ partition by pivots and send each probe row to every partition that may hold its neighbors, in MapReduce~\cite{LuKNNMapReduce12}, Simba does the same in Spark~\cite{XieSimba16}, and SemDeDup compares rows only within their k-means cluster~\cite{AbbasSemDeDup23}, which is our join with $n_p=1$ (Table~\ref{tab:join-analogy}).
What is new here is the GPU form: one GEMM per slice under the exact join's bound, partitions built at query time and sized by the planner.
Recent work targets approximate joins over learned embeddings on the CPU: SimJoin joins two vector sets under a threshold over proximity graphs and argues, as we do, that a join should not run as one search per probe row~\cite{XieSimJoin25}; DiskJoin runs a billion-scale threshold join from an SSD with probabilistic pruning~\cite{ChenDiskJoin25}; Kim et al.\ co-design an offline and an online phase~\cite{KimVectorJoin26}; and SieveIVF prunes the pairs an IVF index need not compare~\cite{HuSieveIVF26}.
These are standalone algorithms with approximate answers; NeMo Curator runs SemDeDup in production~\cite{NeMoCurator}.

\paragraph{Vector joins in SQL engines}
DuckDB, Spark, Databricks and BigQuery express vector joins in SQL, through brute-force macros, HNSW or IVF indexes, a \texttt{NEAREST BY} clause or a table of queries, and run them on the CPU as a per-row top-$k$~\cite{DuckDBVSS, DuckDBVSSBlog24, DuckDBNearestByPR24137, SparkNearestByJoin26, DatabricksNearestByJoinBlog26, BigQueryVectorSearchFn}.
MaxVec adds a two-input vector-search operator to a CPU--GPU relational engine~\cite{MaxVec26}; it searches an index once per probe row, an index nested-loop join (Table~\ref{tab:join-analogy}), reports that GPU search pays off only once the index is reorganized to move less data, and its Vec-H benchmark is the source of our SQL workload.
Sirius instead plans the join over the whole probe side at once, as a nested-loop or a partitioned hash join, and adds threshold and global top-$k$ joins as planned GPU operators; MaxVec's finding holds for its index nested-loop plan, which Section~\ref{subsec:eval-sql} bears out.
A similarity predicate beside a relational one is also the problem of filtered vector search, which indexes such as ACORN answer inside the index~\cite{PatelACORN24}; Sirius leaves the relational predicate to the plan, and Section~\ref{subsec:eval-planner} shows what the planner does and does not do with it.

\paragraph{GPU vector search and GPU databases}
FAISS, cuVS and IVF-RaBitQ build and search brute-force, IVF and graph indexes on the GPU~\cite{JohnsonFaissGPU21, NvidiaCuVS26, OotomoCAGRA24, ShiIVFRaBitQ26}, and Milvus and Kinetica use GPU search inside a database~\cite{MilvusGPUIndex, KineticaVectorSearch}; Section~\ref{subsec:vector-search} covers research systems.
All of them answer independent searches with a per-row top-$k$: cuVS has a threshold search only in C++, as a dense boolean matrix, and none has a global top-$k$; FAISS's \texttt{big\_batch\_search} walks a prebuilt IVF index list by list for a batch of queries, the order Section~\ref{subsec:approx-join} gives the join, whose partitions are instead built and sized in the statement.
Section~\ref{sec:exact-join} compares our kernel with the fused distance-and-selection kernels of cuVS and flyKNNG~\cite{JiFlyKNNG22}, lossy fp16 distances~\cite{CurlessFaSTED25} and probabilistic bounds over quantized codes~\cite{GaoRaBitQ24}.
GPU relational joins go back to He et al.~\cite{HeGPUJoins08} and partitioned hash joins to GRACE~\cite{KitsuregawaGrace83}; we add a vector join to a GPU SQL engine~\cite{Yogatama26} and build its approximate form as a partitioned hash join.
