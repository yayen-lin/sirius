\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A vector join relates two tables of vectors, and we argued that it should be built like a relational join: at query time, on the GPU, and planned by the optimizer.
Sirius's exact join is a blocked nested-loop join that filters 1- and 2-byte codes inside the GEMM under a deterministic bound and re-scores the survivors in fp32; its approximate join is a partitioned hash join whose partitioning function is k-means, run slice by slice with the same kernel.
With every build charged, the approximate join matches or beats every GPU index we measured at every recall from 0.90, by 1.2--4$\times$ at recall 0.95 and 10k probe rows and by an order of magnitude at 10M, and the exact join is 5.9--7.6$\times$ faster than FAISS-GPU's exact search at 10k probe rows, up to 100M rows, after the first statement in a process.
A prebuilt graph index wins only when it serves thousands of small statements, as a persistent index does for relational data; choosing between the two from the catalog, and pushing relational filters through the join, are the planner's next decisions.
